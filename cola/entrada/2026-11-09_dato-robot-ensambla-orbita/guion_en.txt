[scene_001 | 8s]
In orbit, a robot joins pieces with no astronauts beside it. It is Caltech's ASTRA demonstration.

[scene_002 | 9s]
A piece leaves the dispenser; the arm aligns it and locks it onto a floating truss.

[scene_003 | 8s]
ASTRA left Vandenberg, California, on March 30, 2026, headed for low Earth orbit aboard Vigoride 7.

[scene_004 | 9s]
The rocket's fairing limits what can be launched already assembled. A new path is to build the structure where it will operate.

[scene_005 | 9s]
It carried two prefabricated segments, a manipulator, and dispensers with parts. In orbit they deployed those two segments.

[scene_006 | 10s]
Then the manipulator assembled a third segment from composite-fiber tubes and aluminum joints. From the ground, the team sent commands and reviewed photographs.

[scene_007 | 8s]
The design calls for a perforated Kapton film in spring-loaded reflector meshes. That is the plan, not a finished antenna.

[scene_008 | 10s]
The planned reflector is 1.4 meters and needs eleven segments. The demonstration reached the third; finishing the full set before December looks unlikely.

[scene_009 | 11s]
The surprise is not a giant antenna ready to go: it is building in modules where the structure will work. What would you assemble up there? That was Datos y ociosidades.
