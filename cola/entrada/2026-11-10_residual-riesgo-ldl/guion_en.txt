[scene_001 | 9s]
Why does a heart attack still come after bad cholesterol has already dropped? A team at Chalmers, in Sweden, looked for the answer in the blood.

[scene_002 | 9s]
Lowering LDL cholesterol does reduce the risk of a heart attack. But bringing it into the recommended range does not erase it: a residual risk remains.

[scene_003 | 9s]
Cholesterol does not travel alone: particles called lipoproteins carry it. Three types load a protein called apolipoprotein B.

[scene_004 | 9s]
They are LDL, triglyceride-rich lipoproteins, or TRL, and lipoprotein(a). All three can leave cholesterol in artery walls.

[scene_005 | 9s]
The team measured nearly 3,000 proteins in blood and crossed them with genetic data and health records.

[scene_006 | 9s]
The surprise: TRL and lipoprotein(a) showed very similar protein patterns, and those patterns were linked to later heart disease.

[scene_007 | 9s]
Those particles carry proteins that attract immune cells and could inflame the vessel wall, beyond depositing cholesterol.

[scene_008 | 9s]
LDL showed a different pattern, tied to accumulating cholesterol, and lowering it remains the foundation. The study did not prove a treatment or a clinical test.

[scene_009 | 9s]
But there are already clues to why residual risk remains. Which particles should we measure besides LDL? That was Datos y ociosidades.
